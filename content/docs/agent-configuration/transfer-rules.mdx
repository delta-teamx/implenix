# Transfer Rules

Transfer rules determine when the agent hands the call to a human. Every deployment includes a standard set of transfer rules plus custom rules for your business. Well-designed transfer logic is the single most important CSAT factor in AI voice deployments.

## Standard transfer rules (every deployment)

**1. Explicit request to speak with a human.** Trigger: caller says "let me talk to a person," "get me a human," "transfer me to your manager," or equivalent. Action: transfer within 3 seconds, no retention attempt.

**2. Crisis keywords.** Trigger: caller mentions emergency, dying, suicide, safety concern, medical crisis. Action: immediate transfer with context flag to designated crisis handler.

**3. Sensitivity escalation.** Trigger: sentiment turns negative twice, or caller cries, or acoustic signals indicate distress. Action: warm handoff with brief empathetic bridge line from the agent.

**4. Out-of-scope questions.** Trigger: caller asks about something outside the agent's knowledge or scope. Action: transfer to appropriate team with the question flagged for the human.

**5. Comprehension failure.** Trigger: agent asks the same question twice due to unclear caller response. Action: transfer to a human who can hear the accent, understand the phrasing, or clarify context.

## Custom transfer rules

Every deployment adds custom rules based on the specific business. Examples:

**VIP customer rules.** Callers on your VIP list route to their designated account manager immediately.

**High-value opportunity rules.** Callers meeting specific criteria (major donor threshold, enterprise lead size, custom qualification) route to a senior team member.

**Complex-request rules.** Certain intent patterns known to require human judgment (multi-part questions, unusual service requests) route to a specialist.

**Time-based rules.** Different transfer targets for business-hours vs after-hours calls.

**Team-based rules.** Round-robin, first-available, or skill-based routing across your team.

## What happens on transfer

**Warm handoff.** The agent briefly bridges the caller, provides context to the receiving human ("I have Sarah on the line calling about a leaking heater at 412 Cross Street"), and hands the call over. Standard for most transfers.

**Cold transfer.** Agent transfers immediately with no bridge. Used for time-sensitive emergencies where speed matters more than context.

**Message with callback.** If no human is available, agent takes a message with promised callback window. Used as a fallback when transfer targets do not answer.

**Callback booking.** Agent offers to book a specific callback slot on the appropriate person's calendar rather than transferring live. Used for non-urgent transfers where scheduling beats waiting.

## Fallback logic

Every transfer target needs a fallback. If the primary target does not answer within X seconds, the rule specifies the fallback:

- Ring backup person
- Offer callback booking
- Take a message
- Route to voicemail

Untested fallback logic fails in production. Test every one during pre-deployment.

## Transfer analytics

Every transfer is logged with reason, target, outcome (answered, missed, resolved). Monthly review of transfer analytics tells you whether the deployment is drifting or improving.

## Common issues

**Transfers happening too often.** Escalation rules too loose. Tune during monthly review.

**Transfers happening too rarely.** Escalation rules too tight. Callers reaching the wrong scope. Tune.

**Warm handoff feels awkward.** Bridge phrasing needs adjustment. Common tuning target.

**Fallback not firing.** Configuration issue. Test the fallback path specifically.
