Healthcare is one of the few categories where the "AI replacing
doctors" headline is genuinely wrong — and the "AI handling 80% of the
work doctors did not want to do" story is genuinely correct. This
post is the operational breakdown for practice operators.

## Who this is for

Healthcare operators — independent practices through small specialty
groups — evaluating AI voice agents for the operational layer around
patient care. Not the clinical layer. The line is the entire post.

## What the AI does NOT do in healthcare

The hardest line in any AI category. Every Implenix healthcare
deployment respects this:

- **No clinical advice.** No symptom assessment, no medication
  guidance, no "you should" or "you should not." Captures information,
  routes to the right clinical team member.
- **No diagnosis or differential thinking.** The AI does not opine on
  what condition the patient might have.
- **No prescription decisions.** Refill requests captured and routed;
  approval is always a licensed prescriber.
- **No high-stakes triage decisions.** "Should I go to the ER" routes
  to a triage nurse or 911 per practice protocol within 10 seconds.
- **No discussion of test results or imaging findings.** Always to the
  clinician.
- **No conversation about prognosis or end-of-life care.** Routed to
  the appropriate human team member with context briefing.

<Pull>The clean line: AI handles when, where, who, and what insurance. Clinicians handle what's going on with you and what to do about it.</Pull>

## Will AI replace doctors?

No. The agentic-reasoning research at the clinical-diagnosis level is
genuinely impressive in 2026 — but deployed clinical AI lives in
decision-support tools (radiology pre-read, pathology slide review,
clinical-decision-support inside the EHR) not in patient-facing voice
conversations.

What is changing is the operational layer around the doctor: scheduling,
intake, insurance verification, post-care check-ins, refills. That layer
is roughly 20-30% of total practice operating cost, and the AI captures
most of it.

## How voice AI will transform cancer research and oncology specifically

Oncology has three distinct uses where AI voice agents are starting to
land — and where they are not appropriate:

**Where they work:**

- New-patient intake for second-opinion consultations (captures
  records request, insurance verification, oncology-specific intake
  data)
- Treatment scheduling coordination — infusion appointments,
  radiation sessions, surgical pre-ops
- Clinical trial pre-screening — captures eligibility criteria
  (cancer type, stage, prior treatments, biomarkers) and routes
  qualified candidates to the clinical research team
- Post-treatment symptom check-ins on protocols approved by the
  oncology team
- Insurance prior-authorization paperwork coordination

**Where they should never operate:**

- Symptom escalation conversations (acute symptom calls always to a
  triage nurse or on-call oncologist within 30 seconds)
- Test result discussions
- Treatment decisions
- Bereavement / hospice conversations
- Anything touching the clinical content of the cancer journey

The oncology research operational lift is real — particularly clinical
trial enrollment, where intake friction is the single biggest
bottleneck to enrolling eligible patients. AI voice can reduce time-
to-enrollment by 40-60% on protocols that map to a clean intake
script.

## The HIPAA + compliance framework

Every Implenix healthcare deployment operates under:

- **BAA in place** with every client
- **Encryption at rest and in transit** via SOC 2 controls
- **Role-based access** with full audit trail
- **Phone-line recording disclosure** matching state law
- **Data retention policies** matched to clinical record retention
- **Sensitive caller flags** — distressed callers, elderly callers
  with confusion signals, callers asking for emergency help — all
  route to humans within 30 seconds

## What deployment looks like

Healthcare deployments take 18-28 business days due to BAA + compliance
review:

1. **Discovery + clinical-team alignment** (3-4 days)
2. **Build + practice-management integration** (6-9 days)
3. **Clinical-team review** (3-5 days) — clinician reviews and
   approves every script and every escalation rule
4. **BAA + compliance documentation finalize**
5. **Parallel run** (5-7 days)
6. **Cutover** with continued recording review
