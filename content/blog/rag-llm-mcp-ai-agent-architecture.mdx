Walk into any AI agent vendor conversation in 2026 and within ten
minutes someone will say "RAG," "LLM," or "MCP." Most of the time those
words are being used to mean different things by different people in
the same meeting. This post is the plain-English version.

## The three terms in one sentence each

- **LLM agent** — an AI agent powered by a large language model (the
  thing that generates text). Most foundation.
- **RAG agent** — an AI agent that retrieves your specific data
  before generating responses. The thing that makes the LLM actually
  useful for your business.
- **MCP agent** — an AI agent that uses Anthropic's Model Context
  Protocol to call standardized tools. The thing that lets the agent
  *do* work rather than just answer.

Most modern production AI agents are all three: an LLM at the core, RAG
for grounding in your data, MCP for taking actions.

## LLM AI agent: what it actually is

The foundation. "LLM" stands for Large Language Model — GPT-5, Claude
4, Gemini 3, Llama 4. These are the models that generate text based
on the input you give them.

A "pure LLM agent" with no other layers:

- Generates responses based only on its training data
- Has no access to your specific business data
- Cannot take actions (no calendar booking, no CRM updates, no
  payment processing)
- Has a fixed knowledge cutoff (does not know events after a certain
  date)

A pure LLM agent is useful for general questions, content generation,
and education. For a business application — answering your specific
customer's call about their specific order — a pure LLM agent is
insufficient on its own. It does not know your inventory, your
customer's history, or your booking calendar.

This is why every production business AI agent is more than just an
LLM.

## RAG AI agent: what it adds

RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. In plain English: the
agent looks up relevant information from your specific data sources
*before* generating a response.

For a voice agent on an HVAC company's line:

- The LLM provides the conversational understanding ("the caller is
  asking about a service visit")
- The RAG layer retrieves the relevant context (this customer's
  service history, the company's pricing, the dispatch calendar
  availability)
- The LLM then generates a response grounded in that retrieved data

Without RAG, the agent would have to either hallucinate ("I think we
can come Tuesday") or refuse to answer ("I cannot speak to your
specific account"). With RAG, the agent answers correctly: "I see you
had a tune-up in March. Tuesday at 10am is open with Mike, your
regular tech. Want me to book it?"

<Pull>If a vendor sells you an "AI agent" without explaining where the agent gets your specific data from, you're being sold a pure LLM. It will fail in production.</Pull>

RAG matters for three things:

1. **Accuracy.** Reduces hallucination by grounding responses in real
   data.
2. **Personalization.** Lets the agent recognize and respond to your
   specific customer.
3. **Compliance.** Lets you audit exactly what data the agent saw
   when it generated a response.

## MCP AI agent: what it adds beyond RAG

MCP stands for Model Context Protocol. It's a standard (originally
from Anthropic) for how AI agents call external tools — calendars,
CRMs, payment systems, databases.

Before MCP, every AI agent integration was custom-built between the
agent and the target system. After MCP, the integration is
standardized: any MCP-compatible agent can connect to any MCP-
compatible tool.

For a voice agent on a dental practice's line:

- LLM understands the conversation
- RAG retrieves the patient's record
- MCP enables the agent to call the Open Dental booking API, the
  insurance verification webhook, and the Twilio SMS confirmation
  service — all through a standardized interface

Without MCP-style tool calling, the agent can talk about booking but
cannot actually book. With it, the agent completes the action
end-to-end during the call.

If they cannot, they shipped LLM-only and dressed it up as an agent.
