Most local-services businesses know they miss calls. Almost none of them
know what those missed calls cost in dollars. The number is usually 5x
to 20x what the owner guesses, and it is the single most important
input to any decision about an AI receptionist, an answering service, or
just hiring more front-desk staff.

This post is the formula. We will walk through the four variables,
explain how to estimate each one honestly, and show what the number
looks like for three real business profiles. At the end, you can run the
[60-second audit](/audit) and get the same calculation done for you.

## Who this is for

Owner-operators and ops leads at any business where the phone is the
primary inbound channel. The math runs cleanest for local services
(HVAC, plumbing, dental, roofing, vet), but the formula works for
anything from law firms to mortgage brokers to landscape architects.

If your phone rings less than 30 times a month, this is probably not
your bottleneck — you have other problems. Skip this post.

## The formula

It is four variables multiplied together.

```
Missed-call cost = Calls/month × Miss rate × Conversion rate on answered × Avg client value
```

Each variable is an honest estimate, not a measured number. The result
is a working number — directionally correct, not precise. The output
will move ±20% with assumption changes, and that is fine. We are not
trying to compute it to the dollar; we are trying to know whether it is
$2,000/month or $40,000/month.

## Variable 1: Calls per month

This is the easiest variable. Pull it from your phone provider's
analytics or your CRM. If neither has it, count for one week and
multiply by 4.3.

For most local-services SMBs the number lands between 200 and 1,500
inbound calls per month. The shape depends heavily on your marketing
mix — businesses running paid Google with a tracked phone number tend
to be in the 400-800 range; businesses with strong organic and direct
referral often run 200-500.

Be honest. Owners systematically *under*estimate this number because
the calls that land while they are not in front of the phone do not
register in memory. The provider report is the truth.

## Variable 2: Miss rate (the hardest one)

This is the percentage of calls that do not get answered live by
someone who can actually help the caller.

Most owners answer "we miss maybe 10%" — which is wrong, often by a
factor of three. Here is why.

Miss rate has four hidden components, and most owners only count the
first one:

1. **Calls that ring out to voicemail** — the obvious one
2. **Calls picked up but then put on hold and abandoned** — invisible to memory
3. **Calls answered by someone who could not actually help, then dropped** — also invisible
4. **Calls answered after hours by a service that took a message but did not help live** — owners often do not count these

Across 200+ Implenix deployments, the actual miss rate for a typical
SMB before they deploy is 35-55%. This shocks most owners. It is also
why they undervalue the cost: they are calculating against 10% when the
real number is 40%.

If you want to measure this honestly, pull a week of call records and
have someone listen to a sample of 20-30 inbound calls. Score each one
"answered live and helped" vs "anything else." That ratio is your true
miss rate.

## Variable 3: Conversion rate on answered calls

This is the percentage of *answered* calls that turn into a paying
customer. It is the variable most owners track best because it is tied
to revenue.

For local services it usually runs 25-45%. For professional services
(law, accounting, financial advisory) the number drops to 8-20% because
the sales cycle is longer. For retail or commerce it is much lower,
often 5-15%, because most "calls" are not buying-intent.

The relevant number for the missed-call calculation is your conversion
rate on calls *that get answered live*. If you do not measure this,
estimate based on closed jobs ÷ qualified inbound calls last month.

A reasonable default for local services is **35%**. Use that if you have
no better number.

## Variable 4: Average client value

This is gross revenue per closed customer. Use the average over the last
year. If your business has both one-time and recurring revenue, count
the lifetime value of the average customer, not just the first
transaction.

## Working the formula: three examples

### Example 1: HVAC company, 4 trucks

- Calls/month: 600
- Miss rate: 40% (industry-typical, weighted by storm-season spikes)
- Conversion on answered: 38%
- Avg client value: $650

```
Missed-call cost = 600 × 0.40 × 0.38 × $650 = $59,280/month
```

Annualized: **$711,000 in lost pipeline.** Most HVAC owners guess this
number around $40,000-$60,000 *for the year*. The real number is 12x.

### Example 2: Dental practice, 2 chairs

- Calls/month: 350
- Miss rate: 32% (lower because front-desk-heavy operation)
- Conversion on answered: 45% (high — booking is the call's purpose)
- Avg new patient LTV: $1,800

```
Missed-call cost = 350 × 0.32 × 0.45 × $1,800 = $90,720/month
```

Annualized: **over $1.0M in lost LTV.** This number is dominated by
LTV, not call volume — dental's use point is patient retention, not
ring count.

### Example 3: Solo law firm

- Calls/month: 120
- Miss rate: 50% (solo operator, often in court, no front desk)
- Conversion on answered: 18% (consultative sales cycle)
- Avg client value per matter: $3,200

```
Missed-call cost = 120 × 0.50 × 0.18 × $3,200 = $34,560/month
```

Annualized: **$415,000 in lost matters.** Most solo attorneys
underestimate this by 4-6x because they do not realize how many calls
come in while they are in court.

## What the number actually means

The missed-call cost is not the savings number. It is the *upper bound*
on what an AI receptionist can recover. The actual recovered pipeline
is some fraction of it — usually 60-85% based on the deployment
portfolio — depending on:

- How well the AI is tuned for your call profile
- How aggressive your transfer rules are (more transfers = lower AI capture, higher human capture)
- Whether your sales team actually follows up on the leads the AI books

For the working ROI calculation, multiply the missed-call cost by 0.7 to
get a reasonable estimate of what an AI receptionist would actually
recover in your specific business.
